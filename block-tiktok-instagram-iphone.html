How to block TikTok and Instagram on iPhone

Two free ways to stop scrolling: Apple's built-in Screen Time, or a dedicated app blocker for when one tap on "Ignore limit" is too easy.

Block apps free with DetoxScreen

Option 1: Apple Screen Time (built in)

  1. Open Settings → Screen Time → App Limits.
  2. Tap Add Limit, pick TikTok, Instagram or the Social category, and set a daily time.
  3. Turn on Block at End of Limit.

The catch: when the limit is reached you can tap "Ignore limit" and keep going. Setting a Screen Time passcode helps, but only if someone else knows it.

Option 2: a free app blocker that holds (DetoxScreen)

  2. Choose the apps to block: TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Snapchat, Reddit, games…
  3. Pick how: a daily limit (for example 30 min of social media), a few opens per day, a schedule (work, study, night), a place, or a focus session.
  4. Optional: turn on strict mode so the block cannot be loosened and the app cannot be deleted while it's running.

When you open a blocked app, a block screen appears instead. You can take "5 more minutes" a limited number of times per day, so it stays a pause, not a prison.

Blocking only Reels or Shorts

iOS blocks whole apps, not single features. To cut Reels or Shorts, block Instagram or YouTube with a small daily allowance or a few opens per day, and use the website version when you really need it.

FAQ

How do I block TikTok on my iPhone for free?

Use Settings → Screen Time → App Limits for a basic limit, or a free app blocker like DetoxScreen if you want a block that can't be dismissed with one tap.

Why doesn't my Screen Time limit stop me?

Apple's App Limits offer an "Ignore limit" button. Without a passcode held by someone else, it's easy to bypass. App blockers with a strict mode, such as DetoxScreen, remove that escape.

Can I block Instagram Reels but keep messages?

Not separately: iOS apps are blocked as a whole. A common workaround is a few short opens per day for Instagram, enough for messages but not for endless Reels.

Does blocking apps need an account or my data?

Not with DetoxScreen: no account, and iOS never reveals which apps you block to the app.
